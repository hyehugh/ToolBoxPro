## QR Codes in 2026

QR codes are everywhere — restaurant menus, business cards, product packaging, event tickets. A well-designed QR code can increase scan rates by 40% compared to a plain black-and-white version.

## What Are QR Codes?

QR (Quick Response) codes are two-dimensional barcodes that store information in a pattern of black and white squares. Unlike traditional barcodes, which hold data in one dimension (left to right), QR codes use both horizontal and vertical dimensions, allowing them to store significantly more data — up to 3KB.

A standard QR code has three distinctive square finder patterns in the corners that help scanners detect the code's orientation. The remaining area contains the encoded data, error correction bits, and timing patterns that ensure reliable scanning even when the code is partially damaged.

## How to Scan a QR Code

### On iPhone (iOS 11+)
Open the Camera app and point it at the QR code. A notification banner appears — tap it to open the link or perform the action. No third-party app needed.

### On Android (modern versions)
Open the default Camera app, point at the code, and tap the pop-up notification. Samsung phones and Google Pixel devices have built-in support. Older phones may need Google Lens.

### On Desktop
Use a webcam-based QR scanner website, or if the QR code is on your screen, take a screenshot and use a desktop QR reader app.

## Customizing QR Codes

### Colors That Work

The key rule: **dark on light with high contrast**. The scanner needs to distinguish between modules (the dark squares) and the background.

| Color Pair | Scan Reliability | Best For |
|------------|-----------------|----------|
| Black on white | 100% | Maximum compatibility |
| Dark blue on white | 99% | Brand-safe, professional |
| Brand color on white | 95% (with testing) | Marketing materials |
| Dark on light gray | 90% | Subtle, elegant designs |
| Light on dark (inverted) | 60-80% | Can fail on some scanners |
| Low contrast (e.g., gray on gray) | <50% | Not recommended |

Always test your custom-colored QR code with at least three different devices before printing.

### Error Correction Levels

QR codes include error correction to remain scannable even when damaged or partially obscured:

| Level | Recovery | Best For |
|-------|----------|----------|
| L (Low) | ~7% | Clean prints, no logo overlay |
| M (Medium) | ~15% | General use, some wear expected |
| Q (Quartile) | ~25% | Adding a center logo, less-than-ideal printing |
| H (High) | ~30% | Maximum durability, large center logo |

**Tip:** If you plan to add a logo in the center of your QR code, use level H or Q. The logo will cover about 15-20% of the code area, and the error correction will compensate for the missing data.

### Size Recommendations

| Use Case | Minimum Size | Recommended Size | Format |
|----------|-------------|-----------------|--------|
| Website (on-screen) | 150px | 256px | PNG |
| Email signature | 100px | 200px | PNG |

| Poster/flyer | 3cm (1.2in) | 5cm (2in) | SVG |
| Billboard | 10cm (4in) | 20cm (8in) | SVG |

For print, always use SVG (vector) format — it scales to any size without quality loss and produces sharp, scannable codes at any dimension.

## FAQ

**Can I add a logo to a QR code?** Yes — use higher error correction (H or Q) to make room for a center logo without breaking the scan.

**What's the best format for printing?** SVG — it's vector-based and scales to any size without quality loss.

**Do QR codes expire?** No — once generated, a QR code works forever. The content is encoded in the pattern itself. However, if the QR code points to a URL, the URL destination can change or go offline.

**How small can a QR code be?** The minimum size depends on the scanner. With a modern smartphone camera, a 1.5cm (0.6in) code with low data density scans reliably.

**Can I track QR code scans?** Our tool generates static QR codes (data is encoded directly). For tracking, use a URL shortener that redirects to your target — then you can track clicks on the shortener side.

**What's the maximum data I can store?** A QR code can hold up to 4,296 alphanumeric characters or 2,953 bytes of binary data. For anything larger, use a URL type and host the content online.
